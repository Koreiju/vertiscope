## Hands down

Give shape to: the online UMAP model trains on EVERY chunk's vector while that
is affordable, and on page centroids once it is not -- "the former for few
pages and the latter for tens to hundreds" (pg-062) -- with the choice decided
by what an update actually costs on this GPU, and the choice, its reason and
its timing visible.

## Demands back

1. **Decided by measurement, not by a constant page count.** The switch reads a
   cost model of `update()` fed by the updates this GPU actually ran.
   Measured 2026-09-28 (RTX 4070 laptop, 768-d): an update of 10 rows over
   160 / 500 / 1,000 / 2,000 trained rows took 1.5 / 2.7 / 6.6 / 15.9 s.
2. **Observable.** Status and every fit record carry `scope`, why, the rows
   it would train, the predicted and the budgeted milliseconds, and the
   measured costs the prediction came from.
3. **Chunk scope trains on chunks.** In it, `pumap._raw` holds the chunk
   vectors as well as the page centroids, and each new chunk joins the
   training at the next online update -- otherwise "every chunk" is only a
   transform of points the model never saw, which is today's page scope.
4. **Drawing does not change with the scope.** Chunks stay drawn in their
   page's neighbourhood (the jumble rejection, 2026-09-26/27); the scope
   changes what the model learned, not the layout rule.
5. **Page scope is exactly today's behaviour** at tens to hundreds of pages
   (the working store: 159 pages, 16,189 chunks -- chunk scope would cost
   minutes per update there).
6. **A planted arm moves it.** A tiny budget forces pages on a small store; a
   huge one forces chunks on a big one.

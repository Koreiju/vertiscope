## Hands down

Give shape to: a 3D scene that draws, every frame, every node and edge the graph
holds -- including every stored chunk of an open page ([[Q.001]]: *Everything
stored*) -- at a cost that does not grow in draw calls with the number of nodes.
Each node keeps what it looks like today: a coloured sphere sized by its value;
a picture when it has one (the page's favicon, a chunk's largest rendered
image); a translucent sphere while it is streaming; a ring when hovered or
pinned.

## Demands back

Any design must satisfy all of these, or it does not serve the claim:

1. **Draw calls are bounded.** A handful per frame (≤ 10) whatever the node
   count -- 250 or 2,500. Measured today: ~1,800 at 2,467 nodes.
2. **Nothing stored is dropped from the picture.** No culling of stored chunks,
   no history trimming ([[Q.001]]). Level of detail is allowed; omission is not.
3. **Picking still works per node.** Hover, click and right-click land on the
   node under the pointer, as they do now.
4. **What is drawn is what collides.** Each node is drawn at `drawnRadius` --
   the size the collider uses (G-21) -- so no drawn overlap hides behind a
   collider that thinks there is room.
5. **Streaming stays translucent** (G-93) and **pictures keep their look**
   (G-81: favicons on pages, the largest rendered picture on chunks).
6. **Memory is bounded by atlas pages, not by nodes.** No texture per node;
   1,476 textures today.
7. **The frame budget is measured, with a planted arm.** Frame time and draw
   calls at the forum's 2,467 nodes, readable over MCP; the old per-node objects,
   planted back, must fail the same bar.
8. **Rendering reads the layout; it never moves it.** No force, position or
   size is changed by drawing (the D-38 lesson: mutations of the live arrays are
   the pane's, never the renderer's).
